Cancer Malignancy Prediction

Task:

Predictive Modeling

Type:

Academic

Neural networks offer exceptional predictive power at the expense of huge computational costs. Oversimplifying things, a neural network basically performs addition and multiplication on a large set of numbers. Wouldn't it be so much simpler and less computationally-taxing if some of these numbers were small, if not exactly 0? In that case, we would be adding and multiplying 0's which is much easier. For my thesis, I extended the study of de Resende et al. (2024) which made use of regularization terms based on the \(l_0\) and \(l_2\) norms which "encourages" weights to tend towards 0 during training, and extended it to the Adam optimization algorithm. The final network achieved a 98% testing accuracy while boasting sparsity, having 61% of its weights be 0's
